- The kernel now finds and checks its clocks (Phase 1c part 5, step 1c3a). The HPET is started from whatever firmware left (counter halted, every comparator's interrupt and FSB delivery switched off, legacy replacement off, then counting) and must be seen to count; the ACPI PM timer is read three times per reading so a glitched read never reaches a calibration. When both exist the PM timer is measured against the HPET, and if it is more than 500 ppm from its specified 315/88 MHz the HPET's period register is wrong and the HPET is not used. The calibration reference is the HPET, else the PM timer, else the PIT's channel 2. The TSC's frequency comes from CPUID when it is given exactly (leaf 15h or a hypervisor's timing leaf), otherwise it is measured against the reference for up to 100 ms with a proven error bound (typically under 10 ppm), and the clock is an invariant TSC, else the HPET, else the PM timer. The boot log's new `time:` line gives the reference, the TSC's frequency and bound, and the clock.
- New host-tested crate `optimal-time`: frequencies as exact fractions (the PM timer and PIT are not whole numbers of hertz), ticks to nanoseconds as one multiply and shift that is proven within 1 ns of exact and monotonic, a calibration bound that is proven to hold whatever happens between reads (checked against an exact model of two counters with random jitter and phases), and the HPET, PIT, PM timer and CPUID encodings. Every mutant is caught except equivalent ones (OR of disjoint bits), which are listed with the reason.
- Found on the way: QEMU's ACPI HPET table always says 3 comparators even when its HPET has more; the kernel reports the mismatch and uses the hardware's count.
- Tough conditions: no HPET (PM timer reference) on q35, pc, UEFI and four emulated CPUs; no ACPI at all (PIT reference, with the non-invariant TSC warning); HPETs with 8 and 32 comparators; a TSC with and without invariance under KVM; four emulated CPUs skewing each other during calibration. Every tough-conditions and kernel-test boot now also checks the `time:` line. 6 new kernel tests, including the TSC measured against every reference (HPET, PM timer and PIT) agreeing, and time on the TSC matching time on the reference over the same bracketed interval.
