### Kernel: the clocksource watchdog (1c3b2b)

- Once the 10 s refinement is done, the kernel checks the TSC against the HPET (else the PM timer) every second and detects only instability, as ROADMAP 1c asks: the TSC going backwards, or running 500 ppm or more from its frequency over the last 10 checks, from the tightest of 3 bracketed reads per check. "500 ppm or more" means the whole error bound of the measurement lies that far away, so a noisy check (an SMI, a descheduled vCPU) never fires it, and a steady error under the limit is left alone.
- Every reference read has a clock read on each side, so the 24-bit PM timer's wraps (every 4.7 s) are counted exactly whatever stalls the reads; a clock that disagrees with the reference by more than a factor of 2 between two reads is unstable too, and a gap too long to count the wraps restarts the window instead of guessing. What a narrow reference cannot catch (a jump of seconds inside such a gap, or one within milliseconds of a whole number of wraps) is written down in `optimal_time::watchdog`.
- When the TSC is unstable the clock falls back to that reference for good: the clock's counter, frequency and extension now sit behind the same seqlock as its timeline, so reads stay lock-free, and the new timeline starts where the old one was. After the TSC went backwards it starts later still, past every reading made while the TSC still worked. TSC-deadline events become LAPIC one-shot ones, the LAPIC timer's rate is carried across with the TSC's bound against the new clock added exactly, and a narrow new clock gets its housekeeping. A timer event that comes early by the clock runs a check at once, so a clock that went back cannot put its own check off.
- The boot log's new `watchdog:` line says what it watches (or why it cannot: no HPET or PM timer, which is a warning), and a second line says when it fired, why, and what the clock and events are now.
- `optimal-time` gains `watchdog`: the check state machine, the exact 500 ppm test, the wrap count with its reason when there is none, the floor after going backwards and the rate carried across, with hand-worked cases, a simulated clock and reference read with random delays and stalls (every verdict must be true, every rate step of 700 ppm or more must be caught within a window, every backwards step at the next check), and mutation tests.
- Test builds take `tscfake=skew+N`, `skew-N` and `back`, which fake the clock's TSC readings 3 s after the watchdog starts. Tough conditions: 2000 ppm fast and 1500 ppm slow, 3 s back, against the HPET and the PM timer under TCG, fast and back under KVM with TSC-deadline events, and 200 ppm fast, which must be left alone; the whole test suite then runs on the clock it fell back to. Every boot checks the `watchdog:` line.
