- The kernel now has timer events and a clock (Phase 1c part 5, step 1c3b1). The local APIC timer's count rate is measured against the kernel clock's own counter at boot (at most 50 ms, goal 100 ppm, with a proven bound), and events are programmed in TSC-deadline mode when the CPU has it and an invariant TSC is the clock, otherwise with the LAPIC timer counting down (one-shot) at the measured rate. A timer never fires early: armings round up at the fastest rate the bound allows (one extra divided tick for the divider's unknown phase), the interrupt handler reads the clock and re-arms if the deadline has not passed, deadlines longer than one arming are reached in several, and with nothing waiting the timer is left disarmed (no periodic tick). Periodic mode is there for tests and measurement.
- The clock: nanoseconds since start-up from the TSC, a 64-bit HPET (both lock-free) or a narrow counter (the 24-bit PM timer, a 32-bit HPET) extended to 64 bits under a lock, which the timer keeps extended with a housekeeping read every quarter wrap. The boot log's new `timer:` line gives the LAPIC timer's rate and bound, what it was measured against, how events are programmed, and whether the LAPIC timer keeps running in deep C-states (ARAT).
- `optimal-time` gains the LAPIC timer encodings (LVT modes, the divide register's odd bit layout), exact arming (the fewest ticks sure to last a time at the fastest the bound allows, the finest divider whose count fits in 32 bits, TSC deadlines), the clock timeline (monotonic, within 1 ns of exact for any span, and continuous across a change of frequency for the coming 10 s refinement), the event-source rule and the ARAT bit, each checked against exact 256-bit arithmetic with random inputs.
- Kernel tests: every way of arming (TSC-deadline, one-shot, one-shot in 3 ms pieces, periodic) never fires early, checked on the clock, on the calibration reference read independently, and by counting hardware interrupts that came before what the hardware was armed for (there must be none); cancelled and replaced deadlines never run, past ones run at once; the LAPIC timer's rate agrees with a second measurement against the reference; the clock never goes backwards and matches the reference over a bracketed interval; and a 24-bit PM timer clock stays right across two wraps with only the housekeeping reading it.
- Tough conditions: the PM timer as the clock (no HPET, no invariant TSC), an invariant TSC with and without TSC-deadline mode, and every tough-conditions and kernel-test boot now also checks the `timer:` line.
