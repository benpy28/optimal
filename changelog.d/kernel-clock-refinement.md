### Kernel: the clock's 10 s refinement and the CPUID claim check (1c3b2a)

- Over the first 10 s the kernel measures the TSC again against the HPET or the PM timer (ROADMAP 1c), from one bracketed sample at each end with timer events in between, and gives the clock the better frequency without a jump: the clock's timeline sits behind a seqlock, so reads stay lock-free and a rebase is continuous and monotonic. A measured boot value is replaced when the 10 s one agrees with it and is tighter (well under 1 ppm, from tens of ppm); if the two cannot both be right the TSC's rate changed, which is a warning. An exact CPUID claim (leaf 15h or the hypervisor's leaf) is checked against the measurement and kept when it agrees; a claim that is wrong is a warning and is replaced.
- A 24-bit PM timer wraps twice in the window: it is read every eighth of a wrap, and the wraps between reads are counted from the TSC, refusing rather than guessing when more than one count could fit (a vCPU stopped for longer than a wrap).
- The LAPIC timer's rate and the TSC-deadline rate move with the clock exactly, under the timer's lock, so no timer is ever armed with a mix of old and new.
- The boot log's new `refine:` line says what happened (refined, kept, confirmed, replaced, or why it did not run, such as a PIT reference, which ROADMAP 1c does not refine against). With nothing to run after boot, the kernel now idles with interrupts on instead of halting with them off, so the refinement finishes and is reported.
- `optimal-time` gains the refinement's decisions: whether two bounded measurements can both be right, the unique wrap count between two reads of a narrow counter, carrying a rate across a change of the clock's frequency, and the decision itself, with hand-worked cases and random inputs checked against brute force and exact 256-bit arithmetic.
- Test builds take `tscfake=invariant` (the TSC as the clock under TCG, whose TSC is the host's) and `tscfake=claim+N` (a hypervisor claim N ppm off). Tough conditions: the refinement against the HPET and the PM timer under TCG and KVM, a claim faked 2000 ppm off caught and replaced (with one-shot and TSC-deadline events), and every boot checks the `refine:` line.
